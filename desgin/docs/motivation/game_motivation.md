The important thing is the contrast between stupid characters and ridiculously spectacular combat.

Imagine a cactus with an enormous handlebar moustache, tiny cowboy boots, and a revolver that fires bullets the size of refrigerators. His ultimate ability summons an enormous flaming moustache from the sky that crushes enemies while screaming obscenities.

And nobody ever explains why everyone has a moustache. Not even the cactus.

## My initial creative pillars

1\. Completely absurd humour

Enemies have ridiculous personalities, bosses deliver unhinged monologues, upgrades have nonsensical descriptions, and even the revolver might insult your aim.

2\. Combat that becomes progressively insane

Start with a six-shooter. Eventually fire 300 bullets a second, summon cactus tornadoes, ricochet projectiles across the map, and trigger enormous chain explosions.

3\. A bizarre, unpredictable apocalypse

Sentient refrigerators, psychopathic vegetables, muscular pigeons, and mutated politicians. Every major character has an enormous moustache, with absolutely no explanation.

4\. Spectacular, exaggerated animations

Extreme squash-and-stretch, dramatic slow-motion finishers, camera shakes, absurdly oversized weapons, and ultimate attacks that temporarily turn the entire screen into a cartoon spectacle.

## One important gameplay decision

The next thing I'd define is the core progression system, because that determines how the game feels.

Roguelite shooter

Short, intense runs. Choose ridiculous upgrades, die, unlock new things, repeat. Similar to Risk of Rain 2.

Incremental power fantasy

Persistent progression, insane scaling, and increasingly powerful abilities that eventually fill the screen with chaos.

Hybrid: roguelite + incremental (my pick)

Runs with randomized upgrades and increasingly absurd combat, plus permanent unlocks, evolving abilities, and major progression between runs.

Develop the gameplay&#x20;

I would also make the cactus a completely serious, angry little gunslinger surrounded by absolute idiots. That contrast could make him much funnier than having every character constantly tell jokes.


# MUSTACHE MASSACRE

Core Gameplay Design — Concept v0.1

Genre: Isometric action shooter × Roguelite × Incremental power fantasy

Core fantasy: You're a furious little cactus with an enormous moustache and a revolver. You start by shooting deranged wasteland creatures one at a time. Twenty minutes later, you're teleporting between enemies, firing thousands of ricocheting bullets, summoning nuclear tumbleweeds, and fighting a giant sentient toilet with a French accent.

The game should feel ridiculously responsive at the beginning and completely out of control by the end — but the player should always feel responsible for the carnage.

## 1. The core gameplay loop

SHOOT, DASH & DESTROY

Fast movement, manual aiming, absurd enemies

BUILD YOUR STACHE METER

Chain kills, take risks, earn explosive power

STACK RIDICULOUS UPGRADES

Transform weapons, abilities and interactions

BECOME A WALKING CATASTROPHE

Chain reactions, giant ultimates, boss fights

DIE, UNLOCK & RETURN

Permanent unlocks and wildly different new builds

The most important feature: the player is never just watching the game play itself. Even when automatic effects are triggering everywhere, positioning, aiming, dodging and choosing targets remain essential.

Combat should have a strong foundation before upgrades even enter the picture.

The cactus has a revolver with six powerful, satisfying shots, a fast dash with a brief invulnerability window, and a stylish reload animation where he spins the cylinder using his moustache.

I'd also give him an active reload mechanic. Reload with perfect timing and the next cylinder becomes empowered. Miss the timing, and nothing catastrophic happens — you simply lose the bonus.

This creates a rhythm of shooting, reloading, dashing and shooting again.

## 3. The signature mechanic: STACHE FRENZY

This is what could make Mustache Massacre mechanically recognizable.

## STACHE-O-METER

Style-powered combat multiplier

FRENZY CHARGE

72%

Magnificent

Your moustache becomes enormous. Bonus ricochets and faster special-ability charging.

Drag the slider to explore how the player transforms as the meter fills.

You build the meter by aggressively killing enemies, narrowly dodging attacks, achieving multikills, and performing stylish finishers.

The meter gradually decays when you stop fighting. Taking damage costs momentum.

When you activate Stache Frenzy, the cactus's moustache grows to several times his body size. His eyes ignite, the soundtrack intensifies, abilities mutate, and the combat becomes temporarily outrageous.

The crucial design choice is that the moustache changes visibly with your momentum. This makes performance immediately readable and gives the game its own identity.

### Example: a completely broken build

Imagine you have these four effects:

- Bullets ricochet to three extra enemies.
- Critical hits make enemies explode into needles.
- Needles inherit bullet modifiers.
- Every fifth projectile becomes explosive.

You shoot a single enemy. The bullet ricochets. Three enemies explode into needles. Those needles ricochet again, and some explode on impact.

One shot can trigger dozens of secondary effects.

That's the incremental power fantasy I want for this game. The player discovers absurd interactions rather than simply collecting bigger numbers.

There should also be rare Mutations: upgrades that fundamentally change an ability. For example, Tumbleweed of Doom could evolve into a giant flaming wheel that the cactus rides across the arena while shooting two revolvers.

## 7. Permanent progression without ruining the roguelite

I would separate progression into three layers.

| Layer               | What progresses                                                 | Persistence |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Combat upgrades     | Damage, ricochets, mutations, abilities, crazy synergies        | Current run |
| The Grooming Garage | New weapons, upgrade families, characters, game systems         | Permanent   |
| Mastery             | Character challenges, alternate weapon modes, special mutations | Permanent   |

The central hub could be The Grooming Garage, run by a suspiciously muscular barber with a moustache bigger than his torso.

Players collect Moustache Wax during runs, which they spend unlocking additional items, weapons, characters, and more advanced upgrade combinations.

I would avoid making permanent stat boosts too powerful. Most of the permanent progression should unlock new possibilities, not simply make every subsequent run easier. The objective is replayability through increasingly bizarre builds.

To achieve the frenetic appearance you're imagining, I'd establish a visual hierarchy.

Normal attacks should be quick and sharp: exaggerated recoil, stretched bullets, muzzle flashes and expressive enemy hit reactions.

Special abilities should be spectacular: swirling projectiles, enormous cartoon effects, enemies flying around with exaggerated ragdoll physics, and giant impact frames.

Ultimate attacks should feel almost cinematic: brief slow motion, a dramatic character pose, music accents, huge effects and then an enormous release of destruction.

The important constraint is that these animations cannot constantly interrupt gameplay. Most ultimates should be designed around continuous player control, with only exceptionally important events briefly altering time or camera behaviour.

Even when a thousand things happen, enemy danger zones and the player's position must remain readable.

## The key design decision: characters are more than weapon skins

Every character should have four unique elements:

| Mechanic              | Example: Don Espino             | Example: General Coo                   |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Primary weapon        | Six-shot revolver               | Gatling minigun                        |
| Unique passive        | Critical hits accelerate reload | Sustained shooting increases fire rate |
| Mobility              | Quick precision dash            | Short aerial hop                       |
| Frenzy transformation | Ricocheting golden bullets      | Enormous dual miniguns                 |

This creates genuinely different playstyles instead of simply changing the appearance of the bullets.

## How upgrades work between characters

I would divide upgrades into three categories, rather than developing an entirely separate progression system for each character.

Universal upgrades

Available to every character: explosions, chain lightning, critical-hit modifiers, bouncing projectiles, movement effects and status effects.

Weapon-specific upgrades

Don Espino gets extra chambers, fan-the-hammer attacks and special ammunition. General Coo gets additional barrels, heat conversions and bullet storms.

Legendary character mutations

Extremely rare upgrades that fundamentally transform a character's core mechanics. A revolver might become a six-shot orbital cannon, while a shotgun launches miniature black holes.

The really interesting part is that universal upgrades interact differently with each character's weapon.

For instance, an upgrade called Explosive Personality makes projectiles explode. On Don Espino, that creates six powerful precision explosions. On General Coo, it produces hundreds of tiny explosions. On Captain Blubbers, it causes grenade explosions to spawn secondary grenades.

Same upgrade, radically different result.

## Another idea: character-specific Stache Frenzy

Rather than giving everyone the same powered-up transformation, each character's moustache should mutate in a completely different way during Frenzy.

Don Espino's moustache becomes a pair of glowing golden gunslinger wings. General Coo's splits into spinning minigun barrels. Sir Toastington's catches fire. Escargot Joe's moustache grows into two enormous electric antennae.

These transformations should have exaggerated animation sequences and their own mechanical effects.

## My recommendation for the first release

Start with three playable characters: Don Espino, General Coo and Sir Toastington.

They establish three distinct combat archetypes — precision, projectile saturation, and area damage — which are excellent for validating the upgrade-combination system. The remaining characters can be introduced as unlockables once the core combat feels right.

And I'd establish one absolute rule for the universe:

Every playable character has a magnificent moustache. Nobody acknowledges it. Nobody explains it. Nobody questions it.

Even the rubber duck.

The next thing I'd explore is making each character's weapon progressively evolve during a run — so the cactus still uses his iconic revolver at the end, but it has become an absolutely absurd, visually spectacular instrument of destruction.
